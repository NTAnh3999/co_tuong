## How to tell colors apart (deterministically, without guessing)

When creating the room in `/play/online`, explicitly select the "Đỏ" (red) host-color option — don't use "Ngẫu nhiên" (random). This makes the room's CREATOR always red and the JOINER always black, deterministically, every run. Combined with `CHECKMATE_MOVES` always starting with red's move, this means: the creator's page performs moves at even indices (0, 2, 4, 6, 8), the joiner's page performs moves at odd indices (1, 3, 5, 7, 9). No need to inspect the DOM to figure out "who am I" — the room-creation choice already pins it down.

Close both contexts at the end (`await redContext.close()` / `await blackContext.close()`), and anything else you opened, so the test doesn't leak browser processes across runs.

### Scenario 2 — reload mid-turn resyncs correctly

After a few moves into the same kind of game (reuse the setup from scenario 1, or structure it as a continuation — your call on whether this is the same `test()` or a separate one; a separate `test()` with its own fresh room is simpler to reason about and re-run in isolation, prefer that), `await redPage.reload()` partway through (e.g. after move 3 of the 10), then confirm: the board still shows the correct position (same piece count / same last move highlighted, however you can assert that from the DOM), and the SAME player can still make their next move successfully (the reload didn't desync who's turn it is).

### Scenario 3 — offline/restore

Use `await redContext.setOffline(true)` to simulate a network drop on one context mid-game. Confirm the reconnect banner ("Mất kết nối, đang thử kết nối lại...", from T4.6) appears on that page. Then `await redContext.setOffline(false)` and confirm the banner disappears and the page is usable again (e.g. it can still submit its next move once it's genuinely that color's turn again). Keep the sequence of moves before/after the drop simple — you don't need to drive this all the way to checkmate, just prove disconn→reconnect doesn't break the page.

### Scenario 4 — a third context can't join a full room

With a room already at 2/2 members (any room from an earlier scenario works, or set up a fresh minimal one — your call), open a THIRD browser context, navigate it to `/room/<same code>`, and confirm it shows an error state rather than successfully joining (check `/room/[code]/page.tsx`'s actual error-rendering for this case — it's built on `ErrorState`, read what role/text that component renders so your assertion is accurate rather than guessed).

### CLOCK-01 touch point

Somewhere in scenario 1 (no need for a separate test), assert the `ClockDisplay`'s rendered time text is present and looks like a clock (e.g. matches `/\d{2}:\d{2}/`) for at least one player, confirming the online game page actually surfaces live clock state end-to-end — you don't need to prove it counts down accurately under E2E timing (that's `ClockDisplay.test.tsx`'s job, from T4.6), just that it's there.

## Timeouts

Multiplayer flows involve real network round trips (REST + Socket.IO) that single-player E2E didn't have to wait on. Use generous `await expect(...).toBeVisible({ timeout: ... })`-style waits (Playwright's auto-retrying assertions) rather than fixed `page.waitForTimeout()` sleeps wherever possible, but don't be afraid to raise a specific assertion's timeout above the default 5s where a real round trip is involved (e.g. waiting for the OTHER page's `game:state` push to arrive after a move). Note any timeout value you had to raise, and why, in KNOWN GAPS so the orchestrator can sanity-check it against how the real servers actually perform.
