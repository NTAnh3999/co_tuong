# Brief: T6.4 — 25 puzzles from classic mating patterns

## Goal

Author 25 real puzzle content files for the Xiangqi Academy feature (`packages/content/data/puzzles/` is currently empty — only a test fixture exists). Each puzzle is a forced-mate position the player must solve. Unlike T6.3's lessons, puzzle correctness here is NOT a matter of author judgment — **every puzzle must be objectively verified by actually running T6.2's mate solver (`isExactMateInN`/`solveMateInN`, in `packages/content/src/mate-solver.ts`) against it before you consider it done.** That solver is pure TypeScript with zero external dependencies (no Pikafish binary, no network) — you can run it in your sandbox exactly as you'd run any other function.

## Read first

- `packages/content/src/schema.ts`'s `puzzleSchema` — the exact shape: `id` (unique), `title`, `objective` (Vietnamese), `difficulty` (1-5), `initialFen`, `sideToMove`, `solutionLines: string[][]` (at least one line; each line is a sequence of 4-char UCI moves, attacker/defender alternating, starting with the attacker's key move), `allowedAlternatives` (defaults to `[]`), `hints` (Vietnamese, max 3), `source`.
- `packages/content/src/mate-solver.ts` and `packages/content/src/mate-solver.test.ts` — the EXACT correctness contract your puzzles must satisfy, and four already-hand-verified example positions (including a real mate-in-1 FEN you can directly adapt: `'4k4/4r4/9/2R6/4R4/9/9/9/9/4K4 w - - 0 1'` solved by `c6c9`, and a mate-in-2 FEN). Read `solveMateInN`'s and `isExactMateInN`'s doc comments carefully: "mate in N" means the attacker forces a position with zero legal replies within exactly N of the attacker's own moves (so a `solutionLines` entry has length `2N-1`: N attacker moves interleaved with N-1 forced-through defenses), and depth is capped at 3 (`validateDepth` throws outside 1-3).
- `packages/content/src/validate-content.ts`'s `validatePuzzle` — what `content:validate` checks (FEN parses, `sideToMove` matches the FEN's actual side to move, every move in every `solutionLines`/`allowedAlternatives` line is legal and turns alternate correctly). This does NOT check that the line is actually a forced mate — that's what YOU must separately verify with the mate solver, which is the whole point of this task's design (per `.agents/PLAN.md`: puzzles are "guarded by the T6.2 validator, not by author correctness").
- `packages/content/src/__fixtures__/valid/puzzles/fixture-puzzle.json` — shape example only, not real content (it's not even a mate puzzle).
- ADR-018 for the FEN/square/UCI convention.

## How to produce 25 genuinely verified puzzles

You have two complementary ways to get real, verified positions — use whichever gets you to 25 solid puzzles fastest, mixing both is fine:

1. **Hand-construct a recognizable "classic" pattern** (back-rank chariot mate, a cannon mate using a screen piece, a horse-delivers-mate pattern, a trapped-general-in-the-corner pattern, two-chariot ladder mate, etc.), placing only the pieces needed for the idea to work plus both generals and whatever's needed to make the position reachable/legal. Then VERIFY it: write a small script that calls `fromFen`/`isExactMateInN`/`solveMateInN` from `@xq/xiangqi-core` and `./mate-solver.js` against your candidate FEN at your intended depth N. If it doesn't verify, adjust the position (add/move/remove a defending piece, change whose piece is where) and re-check — don't just keep the unverified FEN and hope.
2. **Generate candidates by random self-play** (same technique T1.4/T2.5 already used successfully in this codebase: play random legal moves against each other from the starting position or from a sparser custom start, using `@xq/xiangqi-core`'s `getAllLegalMoves`/`applyMove`), checking `isExactMateInN(position, 1)` or `(position, 2)` after each move to find genuine forced-mate spots, then use `solveMateInN` to get the actual key move(s)/line for that position. This reliably produces positions that are correct by construction — you're finding real mates, not inventing unverified ones.

For every one of the 25 final puzzles:
- Confirm via the solver that `result.solved === true` at your claimed `N` (and, if you want to label it precisely as "mate in exactly N," confirm `isExactMateInN(position, N)` is `true` too — not required, but good practice; it's fine for a puzzle to just be "a forced mate within N" without insisting it's not also mate-in-fewer).
- Your `solutionLines[0]` should be exactly the key move followed by the forcing continuation the solver reports (or an equally valid one you've independently re-verified).
- If `result.keyMoves.length > 1` (more than one winning first move), either pick a different, uniquely-keyed position instead (preferred, since a puzzle with a unique key move is a cleaner teaching tool), or keep it and list the other key moves'/their lines under `allowedAlternatives` so the puzzle UI can accept any of them — your call, but don't silently ignore non-uniqueness.
- Spread difficulty across the range: mate-in-1 positions are easier (`difficulty: 1-2`), mate-in-2 harder (`difficulty: 2-4`), mate-in-3 hardest (`difficulty: 4-5`). Aim for a mix (don't make all 25 mate-in-1).
- Vietnamese `objective` (e.g. "Chiếu hết trong 1 nước" / "Chiếu hết trong 2 nước"), Vietnamese `hints` (1-3 progressively more specific hints, without just stating the answer in hint 1), `source: "Tạo và kiểm chứng bằng bộ giải chiếu hết"` (or similar honest attribution — don't fabricate a fake historical source/book citation for a generated position).
